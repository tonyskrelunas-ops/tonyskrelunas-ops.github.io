Community Resilience

A community that holds together, the way The Council Fire tells it.

A community is not a small nation. It is the other way around — communities become a nation, and many communities make the companies and the governance. So it starts with the family across the canyon, and it goes all the way up.

This page is for anyone deciding how a group of people will govern itself: a town, a region, a company, a board, a nation or a family.

01The Council FireA council around the fire, under the stars. From the art of The Council Fire.

Every culture facing a breaking world kept a council fire.

Elders in the circle, the young in the circle, the children at the edges. When the systems running the modern world reach their limits, the keepers of many peoples' oldest knowledge are drawn to one fire to remember what actually lasts. This is the fiction that sits underneath all of the work on this page.

02MeetingsA circle on a misty hillside at sunrise, a small fire in the middle, one person speaking with open hands.

How meetings can be run.

What they settle on is a meeting any family, neighborhood, town council, company team, school board, nonprofit board or tribe could run next week: a circle with no head to the table, elders in the room, the young in the room, children at the edges, a stick that passes when the matter is hard, and nothing decided the first night.

03GovernmentWater at the shore of the lake, red earth and sage beside it.

A government that fits.

The form has to fit the people. There is no universal council. We do not send templates. We send principles, and then we run the process where a community finds its own form.

04The proofYoung corn in rows on red earth.

A government the community recognizes outlasts one it is handed.

In 1998 the Navajo Nation passed the Local Governance Act, a law I helped write. Four years of drafting, one rejection, one passage, and a law now adopted by 45 of the Nation's 110 chapters. The whole story is set down while the people who were in those rooms are still here to correct it.
