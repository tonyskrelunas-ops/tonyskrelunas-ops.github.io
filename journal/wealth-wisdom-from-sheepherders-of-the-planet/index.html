Wealth Wisdom from the Sheepherders of the Planet

Across the herding peoples of the earth, wealth was never counted in currency but in flocks, dawns, clan bonds, and the stubborn resilience that outlasts every drought. A field guide to the older arithmetic of prosperity.

March 9, 2025

The Rhythm of the Land

A sheep camp is not a place. It is a symphony.

Picture it: horses grazing, cattle lowing, a homestead standing like a steadfast elder, and relatives swapping stories in the old tongue while wrestling a stubborn corral gate. Last week I went home to my family's land, where the air still hums with ancestral lessons and the occasional bleat of a sheep critiquing your work ethic.

As we cleared dung under a hard sun, my cousin joked, "If sheep could talk, they'd demand a union." And yet, somewhere between the sweat and the laughter, we unraveled a truth older than the mesas around us — one the herding peoples of the earth have carried in common, from the Mongolian steppe to the highlands of the Andes to the pastures of the Sahel: wealth here has never been counted in currency. It is counted in prayers offered to the dawn, in the bonds of kin, and in the stubborn resilience of a flock that outlasts every drought.

This is the arithmetic I keep returning to in my books — the older ledger that Indigenous, rural, and transitioning communities worldwide are now being asked to trade away for something thinner. The great question of our age is whether we will let them.

Humor from the Herd

The elders teach a light heart, good thoughts, and humor as survival tools — not luxuries.

The Mongolian herders warn:

"Never trust a sheep that smiles — it's plotting a jailbreak."

Our sheep, though, are philosophers. One stared at me mid-chore as if to say, "You call this a retreat? I've seen slower lizards."

The Science of a Sheepish Serenity

Modern research now confirms what shepherds everywhere have always known:

  • Stress relief. Working the manure lowers cortisol — or simply leaves you too tired to worry.
  • Oxytocin overload. Holding a lamb floods you with calm. Try explaining that to a ram eyeing your lunch.
  • Open-air mindfulness. Studies show that time outdoors can cut anxiety by nearly a third — and raises your tolerance for a goat's rambunctiousness considerably.

The body, it turns out, was built for this older schedule. The longest-lived peoples on earth are rarely the richest ones; they are the ones still bound to land, to movement, to kin, and to a reason to rise before the sun.

Wealth, the Old Way: More Than Wool

My aunt put it plainly:

"The richest ones are those whose kids still know how to butcher, keep a herd, build a home, and learn to live among others."

True wealth here is woven like a basket, tight and load-bearing:

  • Land and legacy. Families with grazing rights, a place kept for ceremony, and fields tended like heirlooms.
  • The clan code. Teach the little ones to greet the dawn with a run, to bless the meal and the day, and to trade jokes sharp enough to make a coyote laugh.
  • Morning gods and their metrics. Rise at five, pray, sweat from a run, sweep the hearth — because the Morning Gods check receipts. "If your hearth is cold," Grandpa warned, "they'll bless your neighbor instead."

In Practice: The Shepherd's Playbook

How to grow wealthy the old way:

1. The 5 a.m. club — the original one. Not for influencers. You are up to chase the flock, to pray, and to outwork the sun. 2. Positivity as armor. "Avoid negative people like rotten mutton," my grandma snorted. "They dull your axe." 3. Ceremony as fuel. Sing the old songs, eat the blue corn instead of chasing the feed, and let laughter be your signal in the wilderness.

Final Wisdom

The Tswana of southern Africa say:

"A man without a herd is a man without a shadow."

And in my own family we say:

"A family without sheep is a family without glue."

So the next time you crave wealth, skip the ticker. Tend a flock, mend a fence, and let a goat steal your hat. Real prosperity was never in the bank. It is in the dirt, the dawn, and the dumb jokes shared over a pen.

Ask yourself the only filter that has ever mattered — will this make the great-grandchildren whole, or hollow them out? The herding peoples answered it long ago, and their answer still stands. It is not too late to keep the wealth and the wisdom home. The fire is still lit. You are not alone; the circle holds.

(And if you doubt it, ask the Morning Gods. But you'll have to wake up early.)
