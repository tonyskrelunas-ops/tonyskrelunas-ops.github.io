Stories I Carry

The Winter That Didn’t Come

A Sámi reindeer herder came a very long way to a meeting because somebody told him there might be people there who knew what to do with knowledge that is no longer true. I have thought about that for years.

August 26, 2026

I want to tell you about a man I met at a gathering a few years ago.

He kept reindeer. Up north, far north. Sámi. He came a very long way to that meeting, and I found out later that he came for one reason.

He said everybody always asks him about the cold. And he said — the cold is fine. We have always had cold. We are the last people on earth who need to be asked about cold.

Then he said the thing.

The problem is that the cold comes and goes now.

Freeze. Thaw. Freeze again. And when it thaws and then freezes, a skin of ice forms on top of the lichen. Two centimetres.

His animals can dig through a metre of snow. They cannot get through two centimetres of ice.

So they starve. Standing on top of their food.

The part I actually brought you here for

His grandfather could read the winter.

Not guess — read. He could stand outside in October and tell you what February was going to do. And he was right, over and over, for sixty years, because that knowledge had been built and tested and handed down for a very long time.

And this man has all of it.

Every bit. He was taught properly. He can recite it. It is correct.

And it is useless.

Because February does not do what it did.

Why he came

He told me he came to that meeting because somebody had said there might be people there who knew what to do with knowledge that is not true anymore.

I have thought about that sentence for a long time. I think it is the actual question of this century, and almost nobody is asking it out loud.

The method is right. The content has expired.

Here is what I would say to him now, and to you.

The knowledge is not wrong. The method is right and the content has expired. Those are two completely different losses, and it matters enormously which one you think you have had.

His grandfather did not memorise February. Nobody can memorise a February. He learned how to read a winter — what to look at, in what order, what matters, what is noise. That is the method. And the method is still perfect.

What is gone is the specific answer.

So his job is harder than his grandfather’s. His grandfather inherited the answer. This man has to inherit the method and rebuild the answer — in one lifetime, while it keeps moving.

That is harder. I will not pretend otherwise.

But he is not starting from nothing.

Which is also your problem

I think that is what he had been feeling, and I think some of you have been feeling it too.

You look at the world your parents handed you and the instructions do not fit anymore. The career advice does not fit. The map for how a life is supposed to go does not fit. The economy they described is not the one you are standing in. And it is easy — it is almost automatic — to decide that everything they gave you was worthless.

It was not worthless. You were given the method. You just have to do the harder job of rebuilding the answer.

That is not a tragedy. That is an assignment.

And you are not the first generation to get it.

Why I tell this one

Almost every climate story available to people right now is grief or blame, and both of them leave a room heavier than it arrived. I have watched it happen. People come in worried and leave worried and ashamed, and nothing has been handed to them they can carry out the door.

This one hands them a job description instead.

It is also the story for the person who feels their whole inheritance has been made obsolete — which, in most of the rooms I stand in, is nearly everyone. The elder who was taught properly and finds the teaching no longer lands. The parent whose advice has stopped being useful to their own child. The professional whose entire field moved while they were being excellent at it.

They are all the Sámi herder. Correct, and standing on ice.

This is one of the stories I carry, and it belongs to the man who told it to me. I tell it the way he told it, and I try to leave the pause in the right place, because the pause is where the work happens. If you have ever wondered what a far messenger actually does, it is this: somebody said a true thing in one place, and it needed to be somewhere else, so I carried it.
