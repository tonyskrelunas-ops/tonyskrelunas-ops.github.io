The Movement

The First Fire

About twenty people came to the meditation. What they got was not a meditation. It was a crossing with a destination, and at the far end they were expected.

September 1, 2026

The first Far Messenger Retreat happened this weekend at 1440 Multiversity, in the redwoods above Scotts Valley. Three days — Friday and Saturday were the work, with our cooks, and the last day was for rest and the drive home.

About twenty people came to the meditation on Saturday. Many of them had been there Friday and came back for night two, which is the only attendance figure I actually care about.

I want to write down what happened while it is still close, because some of it went past what I had prepared, and those are the parts worth keeping.

The journey west

The written plan was a trail up a mountain and back down. What happened instead was a crossing with a destination, and I think that is the whole difference.

A trail is an experience. A crossing with a welcome at the far end is a story the body believes it lived.

We traveled as far messengers, and the cargo was gratitude — carried west to the people who still hold the songs, the prayers, the honor. Who still plant. Not goods. Thanks, carried a great distance to the ones keeping the ways.

The destination was a coastal people. Where the winds start. Where the clouds start. The people at the edge of the water, upstream of all weather — the place the rain that feeds the corn is born.

Each traveler took a pebble from the stream, and felt a song in it pulling west. Then we went. We read the weather the whole way — the breeze, the winds, the clouds — because the watcher and the messenger are the same person in motion. From a mountain we saw through the eagle’s eyes, and then the eagle came closer, the way a relationship deepens when the road is long enough.

We crossed canyons and mountains and desert. We found a spring that had gone dry, and we overcame it — which is the dry-well teaching lived instead of told. West of Flagstaff we were fed at an outpost. And the ancestral visit was not a separate exercise; the wise ones were met on the road, as travelers, the way it always was.

The fourth day

By the fourth day of a crossing something turns, and it turned in that room.

Every element becomes support. Nothing on the road is against you anymore. The same wind, the same insects, the same distance — all of it now carrying you.

The world did not change. The relationship did.

That is the abundance frequency proven in a body rather than argued at a whiteboard. You cannot talk someone into it. You can walk them far enough that they notice it has happened.

And then they were expected

At the coast we were met. By the ones carrying on the ways. The messengers were awaited, the gratitude was delivered, and the circle closed.

The far messenger teaching says the arrival was the rarest event a generation knew — one night, one fire, everything poured in. This let the room feel what it is to be that expected guest. That was the turn that made it land, and it was not in my notes.

The whole way we kept returning to the pebble — the stone in the hand, imagined cool on the tongue, the jaw loose, fingertips at the temples. Every few minutes, back to the body. That is what kept it a road they walked instead of a movie they watched.

I was on it too. Forty-five minutes, and I crossed with them rather than ahead of them. It is the best abundance meditation I have been part of.

The food was part of the teaching

An hour before the stories, the room ate.

Missy is the main cook, and Henry works the line with her. Our cooks are not catering. Food is the second carrier of the same teaching, and if you have ever tried to settle a nervous system on bad food you already know why. The room ate from the world the stories were about — and then heard the stories.

People underestimate this. A body that has just been fed well is a body that can receive something. Half the work of the evening was done at the serving line.

Then the stories

I told the sheep-herding stories and the winter stories. What the sheep meant to us, and to my grandmother — not livestock, but the center of the world. Wealth, duty, kinship, the daily reason to get up before the sun. The room had eaten from that world an hour earlier, so it was not an anthropology lecture. It was the same evening continuing.

I said that out loud, because I think naming it is what made it work: the meditation walked it, the food tasted it, the stories told it. Nobody experienced three events. They experienced one.

Then I told the Warrior Twins, which the plan had benched.

I had held them for a longer container. But the room had just crossed together for forty-five minutes, and a room that has crossed can hold teachings a cold room cannot. So: the twins that are inside each of us, and the truth that we need them both. The monsters that once destroyed the people, and the ones that were left behind — old age, poverty — the remnant monsters every generation still meets.

And the landing: we have the tools to overcome them. Which is the whole weekend’s toolkit, handed back in its oldest frame.

The lesson I am keeping from that: the set list is the map, the facilitator is the country. The plan benched the Twins. The teller overruled the plan, and the teller was right.

What the room took home

Everyone who sat at that fire holds Living as a Far Messenger, the reader made for this first retreat. Seventy pages: the teaching in one sitting, then eleven worksheets to write in over a year, ending with the one sentence you want to survive you, signed and dated.

Everyone who reads it afterwards carries the book that began there. That is not a marketing line. It is just what happened, and I would rather say it plainly.

What I learned

Three things, written down before I lose them.

  1. A crossing needs a receiving endThe version with a destination and a welcome beat the version with a summit and a return, and it was not close. Being expected is the feeling the whole teaching turns on.
  2. Braid the carriers, and say you are braiding themMeditation, food and story as one evening rather than three sessions. Naming the continuity out loud did more than any single piece of it.
  3. Read the room over the run sheetPreparation is what lets you depart from preparation. The cards did their job as preparation, not as scripture.

This was the first one. Three days, and the third one mattered as much as the other two — nobody should cross and then get straight into a car. There will be more, and the field stations are being built to hold them. But the first fire is the one the reader is named for, and I wanted the record of it to exist somewhere other than my own memory.

Ahéhee’ to everyone who came, and came back on the second night.

Tony Skrelūnas is Diné (Navajo) and Lithuanian. He writes The Ancestral Watch — Stone Breath, The Amber and the Stone, The Council Fire and The Far Messengers — and the working reader, Living as a Far Messenger. He is a PhD candidate; his dissertation looks at human and organizational performance through Diné and Lithuanian ancestral knowledge and the Japanese practices he has long studied. He holds a faculty appointment at 1440 Multiversity, where teaching is the center of the work now. He runs at dawn and hikes with a corgi named Roper.
