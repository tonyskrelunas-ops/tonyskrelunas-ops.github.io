Keynotes & talks

The far messengers, told in the room.

What they were. What we lost when we stopped raising them. And what it means for the crossing we are in now. No slides.

The talks

Four shapes, depending on the room.

  1. The keynote
    The Far Messenger Forty-five to sixty minutes. The desert crossing, the ocean crossing, the honoured roles a whole civilisation was organised around, and the moment the well runs dry. Told as a story, because that is how it was always carried. The room usually does not want it to end.
  2. The commencement
    What you are being handed For graduating classes. I gave the commencement address at Northern Arizona University, and to a doctoral graduating class since. It is a different talk from the keynote: shorter, and it ends by putting something in their hands rather than in their notes.
  3. The organisation
    Calm is a built thing For leadership teams and corporate retreats. Why panic is a failure of preparation and not a personality, what the old carriers trained on the ordinary days, and how a team stores calm before the season that needs it.
  4. The institution
    Four capitals, and what comes after extraction For universities, conferences and boards. Governance that fits its people, economies that keep the wealth home, and the honest work of a transition off fossil fuels. This is the one that argues.

The rooms

Thirty years of them.

Auditoriums and gymnasiums, boardrooms and chapter houses, conference halls and open ground at dusk. The talk changes shape for the room. The argument does not.

Where

The audiences have not looked alike.

Which is the point. The far messengers were not one people’s idea — every people on earth raised them, and every audience recognises their own version of it within about four minutes.

If you want one

Write and say what the room is.

Who is in it, what they are facing, and how long you have. I will tell you which of the four fits, or that none of them do.
