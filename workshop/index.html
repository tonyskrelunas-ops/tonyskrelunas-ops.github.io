The invitation

Down into the canyon. Then up into the harvest.

“You have built a life that looks right from the outside. And still, some quiet part of you knows you have been walking in the dark.”

This weekend is a descent — into the canyon within, the place you have been carrying alone — and then the climb back up into the harvest: what all that hard work was actually for. The walks are gentle and always optional. The real journey is quiet, inward, and safe. We open Friday by the creek — water, breath, and a circle that keeps a seat for you.

Regulate first — settle the nervous system so the body finally stops bracing. Then everything the ancestors left us becomes usable: the breath, the gratitude, the stories, the fire. You come down carrying something heavy. You go home lighter, steadier, and more yourself.

A change of frequency

Three days to shift your whole state.

This is not a conference you take notes at. It's an energetic reset — three days that slowly change the frequency you live at. The redwoods do half the work: standing beneath the Mother Tree, walking the creeks, eating food that is grown, cooked, and blessed as medicine. Your body remembers what calm feels like, and your nervous system finally lets go.

Into that stillness we bring the tools — meditation, breathwork, and sound healing, and wisdom braided from three cultures: the Diné of the red-earth country, the Baltic song-keepers, and the Japanese discipline of stillness. Different tongues, one human operating system. You arrive carrying the noise of a world that feels like it's coming apart. You leave carrying a frequency of well-being you can keep.

The world is loud and fast right now. This is three days of remembering how to be well inside it.

Who comes to the canyon

This weekend is for you.

The woman who gives everything — to work, to family, to everyone — and quietly wonders who refills her.

The one in a hard passage — a loss, a divorce, an empty nest, a career turning over — who needs a safe place to set it down.

The daughter finding her footing — younger, at a crossroads, looking for ground to stand on.

The elder — with a life's worth of wisdom, ready to gather it and pass it on.

The seeker of any kind — anyone who's been walking in the dark and wants to remember the way home.

You don't need to be fit, or a hiker, or experienced in any of this. Many arrive on their own and leave with a circle. Just eighteen guests, so no one gets lost. The oldest medicine is intergenerational — grandmother, mother, daughter, moving together.

Ancient technologies for modern lives

A lineage the grandmothers protected.

The Canyon Within is an intimate three-day gathering guided by Tony Skrelūnas — 1440 adjunct faculty, Diné and Lithuanian author of the novel Stone Breath. Over the weekend you learn breathwork, gratitude, guided visualization, storytelling, and reflective tools — refined across generations, supported by contemporary science, each one simple, portable, and built to live inside your daily life.

The Diné are a matrilineal people, and this knowledge was carried to Tony by women — his great-grandmother Masan, and the grandmothers who kept the practices alive when the world told them to forget. It is woven together with meditation, qigong, sound baths, forest walks, tai chi, and a specially designed, seasonal healing diet — and with the Ancestral Kitchen, where a Navajo family turns the harvest into medicine you can taste.

Food is medicine

The Ancestral Kitchen.

Every meal is part of the healing — a specially designed, seasonal diet, locally sourced, cooked and blessed as medicine, from Friday dinner through Sunday lunch. At its heart is the Ancestral Kitchen, led by Missy & Henry Webster — a Navajo family from Gray Mountain, Arizona. They turn the harvest feast into a hands-on teaching: blue corn, juniper ash, frybread — the old knowledge carried hand to hand. In the Ancestral Kitchen, cooking is a lineage you can taste.

Before you come

Questions, answered.

Do I need to be fit, or a hiker?

No. The descent is inward. The walks are gentle, unhurried, and always optional.

Can I come on my own?

Yes — many do. With just eighteen guests, you'll leave with a circle. It's a safe, warm container.

Can I come for just part of it?

Yes. Day passes are available for Friday evening, Saturday, and Sunday morning — join for the parts that call you, even if you can't stay overnight.

Do I need experience with meditation or breathwork?

None. Everything is taught from the beginning. The first tool, Stone Breath, takes about a minute to learn.

Is this religious?

No. It's ancestral and human. Take what serves you and leave the rest.

What should I bring?

Comfortable clothes and layers, a water bottle, and an open heart. A journal and your stone are provided.
