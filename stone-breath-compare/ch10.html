The Third Arrow – A Hard Truth

The Third Arrow – A Hard Truth

That evening, the three of them sat in a small conference room at the training center. Gintarė looked better – not fixed, but calmer. The stone was in her hand. "I have to tell you something hard," Tonas said. "Not to hurt you. To help you." Gintarė nodded. "In Japan, they teach about the three arrows. The first arrow is what someone does to you – the words that cut, the rumor, the attack. The second arrow is your reaction – the anger, the replaying, the sleepless nights. The third arrow is what you do because of your reaction. Gossip back. Undermine. Quit. Self-destruct." He leaned forward. "The women in the stands are shooting the first arrow. You can't stop that. But you can stop shooting the second and third arrows at yourself. Every time you replay their words, you're pulling the second arrow. Every time you doubt your own talent, you're pulling the third." Gintarė's eyes filled with tears. "How do I stop?" "You don't stop the feelings. You just stop acting on them. You feel the envy, the fear, the shame – and you breathe. You hold the stone.
