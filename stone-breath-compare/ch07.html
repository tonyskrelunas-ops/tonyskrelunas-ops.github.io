Day One – The Gathering

Three days later, forty-seven people showed up. They came from Antalieptė and Padustis and half a dozen smaller villages Tonas had never heard of. They came with tools and food and children and questions. They came because the word had spread – talka – and something in them woke up.

Austėja stood at the edge of the crowd, stunned. "Forty-seven. I haven't seen forty-seven people in this town since – I don't know when." Tonas smiled. "Told you." "You didn't tell me. You just believed." "That's the same thing."

Day Two – The Teaching

Day Two – The Teaching

The roof was framed by midday and the foundation was braced with concrete, and the talka was not just fixing a building — it was rebuilding the village's sense of itself.

The roof was framed by midday. The foundation was braced with concrete. The talka was not just fixing a building – it was rebuilding the village's sense of itself.

At lunch, Rytis found Tonas sitting alone near the river. "You look like a man trying to solve a puzzle." "Maybe." "What's the puzzle?" Tonas was quiet for a moment. Day Three – The Singing and the Dance

The tables were spread with food — casseroles, breads, pickles, meats — and then the old women began to sing. Not loudly. Not for performance. They sang the way Masan used to hum while she ground corn, the way Ada sang to the sheep when she thought no one was listening, the ancient songs, the dainos, the kind you sing among the grass and among the animals as you do your chores. Tonas closed his eyes and the sound wrapped around him, and it was the sound of people who had buried their dead, lost their land, watched their language nearly disappear, and kept singing anyway. He thought of home — the summer social dances after the harvest, when the lambs were growing and the corn fields were tall and green, the long days, the whole community gathered, the old ones singing while the young ones danced. Not ceremony. Just joy. Just being together. Same thing, he thought. Different words. Same thing.
