Why this, why now

A cousin's confirmation, not a competitor.

India carries one of the oldest, deepest wisdom traditions alive — yoga, Ayurveda, the reverence for elders and for the earth as mother. Tony Skrelūnas is Diné and Lithuanian, raised by his grandparents inside the old tools — and a lifelong student of meditation and of our universal cultures. On his first visit to the Hare Krishna temple in Silicon Valley — a monk he'd met downtown had warmly invited him — he came early one morning, joined the kirtan, shared the prasadam, and loved every second. He can't wait to return. The resonance is unmistakable. In both his world and yours, the guest is holy — अतिथि देवो भव, atithi devo bhava — and no one is allowed to leave without eating. The morning prayer of the Diné and the morning puja; hózhó and dharma (धर्म); walking in beauty and living in ṛta — these are the same human operating system, remembered on two sides of the earth. This work doesn't compete with your tradition. It confirms it.
