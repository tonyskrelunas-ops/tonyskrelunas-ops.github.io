## Body

[First Name],

*Stone Breath* went out into the world this week. It is on Apple Books and Kobo in every
territory they serve, at Thalia in Germany, at Angus and Robertson in Australia, at Vivlio
in France, and it is entering the catalogs that public libraries order from. One link finds
whichever store is yours: **books2read.com/u/4AEDdd**

That sentence was a long time coming, and you have been here for some of it.

I want to tell you where the book came from, because most of you know the writing without
knowing the ground underneath it. I was raised in a hogan on Big Mountain, learning to read
by lamplight after the chores were done. My great-grandmother Masan taught me how to breathe
when the work was long and how to begin a morning so that the morning did not begin me, and
she never once called any of it a practice. It was simply how you got the day done.

My Lithuanian grandfather drove a long stretch of dirt road to sit in that same hogan and
hand me what his people had kept through forty years of a ban on their own alphabet. Japan
arrived by mail, in books, read by that same small flame. I have studied it ever since
without being able to claim it as inheritance, and the honest word for what I have with it
is study, not blood.

Three peoples who never met, and the same answers.

*Stone Breath* is a novel built out of all three. Tonas Yazzie has everything the world knows
how to measure, and when his grandfather dies he carries a book of old songs to a Lithuanian
village he has never seen. The village is drawn from a real one called Antalieptė, and the
songs are the dainos my own family carried across an ocean the way other families carried
silver. There he meets Austėja, who trained in the kitchens of Paris, who conditions
athletes, who runs every dawn, who stayed when everyone else left, and who does not need him.
Two people successful by every measure the world still keeps, except the oldest one, which is
whether you can carry what matters across hard ground and arrive with it whole.

Around that story the book carries twenty-two tools, one to a chapter. They cost nothing and
they have been tested for a thousand years.

People ask why I wrote it as a novel instead of the manual it could have been. The answer is
in the sheep camp. Nobody handed you instructions there. They told you what happened to
someone, and the teaching sat inside the telling, and years later, standing in some
difficulty they never saw coming, you would find the story already waiting ahead of you. A
lecture asks the mind to agree. A story asks the whole person to walk somewhere.

If you would rather have the practices without a novel around them, there is now a working
reader called *Living as a Far Messenger*: seventy pages, eleven worksheets, made to be
written in rather than read once. It is at **ancestralwatch.com/reader**

And the things that have always been free are still free. The whole first chapter of
*Stone Breath* is at **ancestralwatch.com/read**, and the tools themselves, including the
stone breath and its printable card, are at **ancestralwatch.com/tools**

Some of you signed up in 2019 and have been reading quietly ever since. I have never written
often, and I would rather send you one thing worth the interruption than keep to a schedule.
Thank you for being here for the long part of this.

Walk in beauty,
**Tony**

*Hózhó · Darna · Wa*
