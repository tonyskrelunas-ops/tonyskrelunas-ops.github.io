On the ground

Built, run, and handed on. Not theorised.

Thirty years of this work has been done inside real institutions, with real budgets and real staff, in rooms where the decision had to hold afterwards. Here is the record.

  1. 14 years
    Founded and directed a national nonprofit program Started it in graduate school and ran it for fourteen years. It grew into the organisation’s largest program by both staff and budget.
  2. 7 years
    Head of commerce for a nation, overseeing 55+ staff Four of those years spent leading the governance reform itself — a restructuring of how a still-deeply-cultural nation governs and does business, begun twenty-five years ago and still standing.
  3. 55
    Communities now certified under the Local Governance Act he co-authored Passed in 1998 on the second attempt, after a public policy process that put elders, youth and leaders in the same room and studied the old systems on nights and weekends. Half of all the communities in the nation have since certified under it — taking on zoning, local revenue and the authority to run their own projects, where before they could only pass resolutions.
  4. 180
    Convened the first Southwest American Indian Tourism Summit One hundred and eighty people in the room. It became the Arizona American Indian Tourism Association, which he co-founded and served as its second president.
  5. 10 years
    Intertribal gatherings — twenty of them, two a year Up to eighty people at a time. Song, running, sweat, food, dance, story. A decade of them, with real healing impact on the communities that hosted them.
  6. 3 years
    Organised and facilitated healing conversations at Rancho La Puerta Three years running the work with elders at one of the oldest wellness destinations in the Americas.
  7. 2025
    A three-day wellness gathering in Sedona Food as medicine, horses, hiking, sacred yoga, sound, movement, running paired with a change of diet — organised and led end to end.

Faculty at 1440 Multiversity · PhD candidate · MBA · inducted into a college of business hall of fame — the first person of Lithuanian and Diné descent to be.

The comeback

College handed him an identity crisis and an inferiority crisis. He finished top of his class.

Raised by his great-grandmother in a hogan on Big Mountain — no electricity, four hundred sheep, a ceremony every dawn. He learned to read by kerosene lamp, and by that same lamp read the philosophy of Japan, a discipline he has been apprenticed to ever since. By choice, not by blood.

Then came the turn: he could be who he was. He finished first. Everything since has been built on what that year taught him: capacity is not inherited, and it is not a personality. It is built.

The braid

Three traditions. Not a blend — a braid.

Two came by blood and one by choice. Each carries a discipline the other two do not. Kept separate, kept whole, and put to work together.

By blood

Diné

Dawn practice, corn pollen, the four sacred mountains, and a way of counting wealth that has never once been in dollars. Raised in it, in a hogan on Big Mountain.

By blood

Lithuanian

The forest, the song, and the stubbornness of a people who were supposed to disappear four times and did not. Endurance as an inherited technology.

By choice

Japanese

Read by kerosene lamp at fourteen and apprenticed to ever since. Restraint, repair, and the discipline of doing one thing completely before starting the next.

The tools for endurance, belonging, and purpose are not lost. They are dormant.

The premise of thirty years of work

The framework

Four capitals. Most organizations count one.

Every decision spends from four accounts. Run three of them into deficit and the fourth eventually collapses too — in a company, in a community, in a person. The work is learning to read all four.

01

Financial

The account everyone already measures. It holds longer when the other three are solvent.

02

Relational

Trust, reciprocity, the people who show up. The slowest to build and the fastest to spend.

03

Cultural

Story, language, ceremony, memory. What tells a people who they are when conditions change.

04

Ecological

Land, water, air, the living systems underneath. The balance sheet no quarter can escape.

The weaving

Yeis standing between corn stalks. Corn pollen is how a Diné day begins — before the coffee, before the phone, before anything is asked of you. Four capitals, woven by someone who was counting them long before anybody wrote them down.
