The signature intensive · live, with me in the room

The Far Messenger.

Everything you know reached you because somebody carried it. Every story your grandmother told you, every song, every remedy, every way of doing a thing right — none of it grew where you found it. Somebody crossed hard ground to bring it.

The skill we are losing is not the stories. It is the carrying. Three days on how it was done, and how you do it now.

September 18–20, 2026 Friday evening · Saturday morning & afternoon · Sunday morning Four sessions · 75 minutes each · short onboarding first

Why this oneOf everything I teach, this is the trunk

I gave this talk once and it ran two hours on its own. The room would not let it end. Other teachings hung off it without being forced — the Coyote stories, the humour, the nervous system, the council.

That is not a session that went well. That is a framework announcing itself. The Far Messenger is not one lecture among three. It is the trunk, and everything else I teach is a branch of it.

You do not make the calm. You retrieve it. The crossing was never made out on the sand — it was made before, in the quiet days, when nobody was watching.

The five thingsWhat you leave holding, in your hand

  1. Somebody carried it.Every story, every song, every remedy, every way of doing a thing right. None of it grew where you found it. Someone crossed the largest sand desert on earth on foot. Someone lay in the hull of a canoe at night, reading the swells through the wood with their own body. They did that so you could know anything at all.
  2. The skill we are losing is not the stories — it is the carrying.The calm heart that crosses the killing ground. The eye that reads a changing sky in time to move. The one who holds the knowing through the breaking and hands it forward.
  3. You do not make the calm. You retrieve it.Panic is not a mood. It is a failure of preparation. And you cannot build the container while the house is burning.
  4. No messenger ever stood alone.Each one sat at the top of a whole civilisation organised around raising honoured people — the weather-reader, the seed-keeper, the one who knew which root fed you and which lied. In that world you rose by mastering something and serving your people with it. Your rising was not a threat. Your rising was your family’s glory.
  5. Two hundred years from now, somebody will be sitting in the dark.They will need exactly what we are holding right now. Where will their stories come from? From us, or from nowhere. We are the far messengers now.

Before we beginThe reading, and why there is reading

This is an intensive. You arrive prepared.

When you take a place I send you three things: The Council Fire, The Far Messengers, and a study manual built for the course.

The manual is not homework. It is the shortest road into the two books — what to read, in what order, and the handful of questions to sit with before Friday. Four seventy-five-minute sessions is not long. It is long enough only because nobody walks in cold.

Before any of that, there is a short onboarding. It takes very little time and it is the part most people skip in every course they have ever taken, which is why most courses do not land.

  1. First, settle yourself.The Stone Breath, before you open a book. Three minutes. A mind running at alarm cannot take anything in and cannot hand anything on — it can only brace. You do not make the calm at the moment you need it. You store it beforehand. This is the whole argument of the course, practised on the course itself.
  2. Then learn how to study again.Not skimming, not highlighting. One sitting, one hour, no phone, paper beside you. How our people actually learned — by lamplight, slowly, and out loud where it mattered. I will show you exactly how to read these two books so they stay with you.
  3. Then learn how to think about it.What to do with a passage that lands on you. Where to stop. What question to carry into the next day rather than answer on the spot. Most reading fails not because the book was wrong but because nothing was ever done with it.
  4. Then prepare.A short list of what to bring: a stone, paper, a pen you like, and one honest answer to one question. That is all the preparation there is, and it is enough.
  5. Then you are ready to carry something.Which is the point. Not to have read two books. To become someone the next person can receive something from.

The novels do the carrying. The sessions do the work. The onboarding is what makes you able to receive either one.

The shapeThree days, four sessions, seventy-five minutes each

A lecture, a writing room, a circle, and the work in the body.

  1. FridayEvening · 75 min

    The Lecture — the story

    Story only. No exercises, no worksheets. I tell it, and the room takes it where it goes. The desert crossing, the ocean crossing, the honoured roles, the Coyote stories in the middle because the room needs to breathe and the laugh makes the next part land harder.

    We close on the packet, and one sentence: tomorrow, we write.

  2. SaturdayMorning · 75 min

    The Writing Room — the prompts

    Not a lecture. Paper, pens, and long silences. I give a prompt, you write for eight minutes, a few read aloud. I say almost nothing and I never comment on the writing. Five prompts: the inventory, the quiet days, the role, the Coyote, and the Fifth World story.

    The last one asks for a single paragraph — the opening of the story somebody will need two hundred years from now. Most people are still working on it a year later.

  3. SaturdayAfternoon · 75 min

    The Circle — guests, sound, stillness

    Guest teachers join us for this one. Sound, meditation, and the practices that settle a nervous system before it needs settling — carried by people who do this work full-time, not by me talking about it.

    This is the session that puts the teaching in the body rather than the notebook.

  4. SundayMorning · 75 min

    The Practicum — the work you take home

    The Stone Breath, in the body. The Ancestral Council — the practice of crossing over and sitting on the other side, and letting the ones who came before council you. Then the container you carry out.

    You leave with a stone, a page, and a first paragraph.

What you keepAfter the last session ends

Two books

The Council Fire and The Far Messengers. Both are yours, included.

The study manual

Sent ahead of the first session, with a short onboarding: how to settle yourself, how to study, how to think about what you read, and how to arrive ready.

The packet

One sheet, both sides. The five things and the five prompts. People keep it for years.

The recordings

All four sessions, yours to keep. Watch them again in a harder week.

The Stone Breath

A three-minute nervous-system reset, in the body rather than in theory.

A first paragraph

The opening of the story you are the one to carry. Most people are still writing it a year later.

Who it is forAnd who it is not

People carrying something. Founders and executives who have spent twenty years trying to manufacture calm at the moment of impact. Parents deciding what actually gets handed down. Writers, teachers and community leaders who can feel that something is being lost and cannot name the mechanism.

You will never be asked to mimic a tradition that is not yours. You take the timeless principle out of the time-bound practice, and adapt it to your own life. Every people on earth was indigenous somewhere, and this is about your own line, not mine.

If you rose — right now, higher than you have ever been — whose glory would that be? If the honest answer is only mine, sit with that. It is not a moral failing. It is a diagnosis of the world you were raised in.
